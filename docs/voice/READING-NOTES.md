# Matchstick voice study

7 October 2026 · Primary-source reading · Editorial research, not model training

## What was actually read

Eight Dave Barry pieces: six complete visible column bodies and selected sections of two longer pieces. Six P. J. O’Rourke publisher excerpts, plus his author-site essay. These are not eight books, complete collected works, or a fine-tuning corpus. Search results, promotional descriptions and inaccessible pages do not count as readings. No full copyrighted texts or copied jokes are stored here.

The compact observations below are original craft notes. Column dates can differ from archive update dates. These are literary references, never evidence about Wadsworth.

## Translation into Matchstick’s own craft

This is an editorial synthesis across the readings, constrained by Matchstick’s reporting job:

- Start with the resident’s concern. Identify the useful tension in the actual facts before choosing a comic device.
- Write from a practical viewpoint: people have errands, children, businesses and budgets. They are allowed to find institutional language peculiar.
- Make a mechanism visible through a familiar object or task. Preserve its status, limits and cost.
- Let the amusing thought live inside the explanation. A story should still have personality when its last sentence is removed.
- Use precise nouns, a surprising sentence ending, varied rhythm and occasional short sentences. Leave space around the turn.
- Be fallible and affectionate. Warmth, pleasure and tenderness belong alongside scrutiny.
- Keep one premise small enough for a brief. A long comic digression can work in a column and still be wrong for a 90-word local story.
- Make a judgment about writing quality. Shorter bureaucratic prose is still bureaucratic prose.

## What does not transfer

Their invented dialogue, imaginary events, reported personal scenes and exaggerated factual assertions cannot become Matchstick reporting. Neither can unverified motives, partisan conclusions, stereotypes or signature routines. Clearly figurative language can explain a sourced fact; it must not masquerade as evidence. A useful, serious school or safety story needs care, not a joke requirement.

Chris’s supplied Chick-fil-A brief remains the local calibration benchmark. It connects access-road mechanics to the ordinary goal of getting lunch. Its inclusion is writing guidance, not a freshness exception for the August milestone.

## How this research reaches publication

Reading notes are supporting documentation, not extra runtime reading. No new model, pipeline stage or paid service was added. Quality remains a judgment that must be demonstrated in actual drafts; updating a prompt is not proof of improved published stories.
